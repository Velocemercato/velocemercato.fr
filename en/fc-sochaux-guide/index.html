FC Sochaux-Montbéliard, France’s factory club

Véloce Mercato · 22 November 2026

A team founded by Peugeot in eastern France helped shape the professional game. Its history still explains much of what you see at Stade Bonal.

How a carmaker helped change French football

Jean-Pierre Peugeot started FC Sochaux in 1928. The club merged with AS Montbéliard, giving it the name you see today. It was an early advocate of paying players openly and launched the Coupe Peugeot, a tournament that preceded the professional French championship established in 1932.

Sochaux won the league in 1935 and 1938 and lifted the Coupe de France in 1937. If you’re new to French football, this is a good place to start. The story of professionalism in France runs through an industrial town near Montbéliard as well as the stadiums in the biggest cities.

Why supporters talk about the Lionceaux

Lionceaux means lion cubs. The club uses the nickname in its account of developing young players. Its academy moved to Seloncourt in 2000, and players such as Benoît Pedretti and Pierre-Alain Frau came through around the time the senior team returned to the top division.

In 2004, Sochaux beat Nantes on penalties to win the Coupe de la Ligue, France’s former league cup. The Coupe de France is a separate competition. Sochaux won that again in 2007, 70 years after its first victory.

Following the club from abroad

For a first trip, check the club’s match listing and its guide to getting to Stade Bonal. The club lists Chabaud-Latour / Joffre as the nearest bus stop. Check the venue and kick-off time before you travel, particularly for cup ties.
